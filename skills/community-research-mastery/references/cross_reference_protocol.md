# Cross-Reference Protocol

The triangulation discipline. Donny does not greenlight a creative bet, hook, or product test from a single-source finding.

## The rule

**1 source = anecdote. 2 = pattern. 3 = signal.**

| Confidence | What it means | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| **None** | No triangulation done | Do not bet money. Do more research. |
| **Low** | 1 source confirms | Anecdote. Worth flagging, not betting on. |
| **Medium** | 2 sources confirm | Pattern. Test small ($100-300 ad spend) to validate. |
| **High** | 3 sources confirm | Signal. Greenlight at full test budget. |

## Mandatory cross-checks per question type

### "Should I run this ad angle?"
1. Reddit pain language present (verbatim quotes)
2. Amazon 1-3 star reviews echo the same complaint
3. Google Trends shows search interest for the language is flat or rising

All 3 → ship. 2 of 3 → test small first. Less → keep researching.

### "Is this product worth testing?"
1. Reddit/community sentiment (people want this category)
2. Amazon review count + star distribution on competing products (real demand exists)
3. Google Trends (interest direction)
4. Optional: Meta Ad Library (competitors actively spending) — see `dropship-competitive-intel`

### "Will this hook convert?"
1. Reddit comment uses the EXACT phrase or close variant
2. Same theme appears in Amazon reviews
3. Optional: similar hook used by an actively-spending competitor

## Independence rule

Sources must be **independent**. Two Reddit threads ≠ two sources. Reddit + a Reddit post on Twitter ≠ two sources. Real independence:

- Reddit
- Amazon reviews
- YouTube comments
- TikTok comments
- Quora
- Niche forum
- Google Trends data
- Meta Ad Library (active spend = market validation)
- Direct customer surveys (later, when you have a list)

## When sources disagree

Reddit says people complain about X. Amazon reviews say people praise X.

Possible explanations:
- Amazon reviews are inflated by Vine/incentivized reviews (filter to Verified Purchase only)
- The complaint is recent; the praise is older
- Different segments use different platforms — Reddit-skewing demo cares about something the Amazon-skewing demo doesn't
- Astroturf on one platform

Do not pick the rosier source. **Disagreement is itself a signal.** Often it tells you the niche is bifurcated and you need to choose which segment your ad targets.

## Quick triangulation runbook (30 minutes)

For a candidate angle/product:

1. **5 min** — `site:reddit.com "<pain phrase>"` Google search → skim top 10 results
2. **5 min** — Amazon: search competing product, sort 1-3 star reviews "Most Recent" → skim 30
3. **5 min** — Google Trends: pain phrase, 5y default; pain phrase + product name comparison
4. **5 min** — YouTube: search "[problem] tutorial", scroll comments on top video, look for pain framing
5. **10 min** — synthesize: write 3 sentences per source, declare confidence label, decide go/no-go

If any of the first 4 steps comes up empty, that's your answer — the signal isn't there.

## What this protocol refuses to allow

- Greenlighting from one source ("I saw a viral TikTok comment thread, let's test")
- Greenlighting from sources that aren't independent (multiple Reddit threads count as one source)
- Skipping triangulation because "the Reddit signal looked strong"
- Betting on disagreement-pattern niches without naming which segment you serve

## When to break the rule

Almost never. The one exception: a competitor is actively spending $$$$ on a single ad angle (see Meta Ad Library) AND the angle uses copy that obviously came from VOC mining. In that case, the competitor has done the triangulation for you and is paying for the test. You can fast-follow at lower risk. But verify the angle is still active and not in fatigue.
