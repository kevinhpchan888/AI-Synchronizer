## 1. The Property {#the-property}

**Imprint name:** TBD (working name: "Honey Hour Stories"; alternatives under consideration include "Late Light," "Kitchen Table Stories," "The Soft Shift," or something entirely new). Throughout this document, `[IMPRINT]` is used where the final name would go. The system works regardless of what it is called.

**Sub-tags (template):** "A [IMPRINT] Short" (Reels), "A [IMPRINT] Episode" (YouTube 3-10 min)
**Parent brand:** AgingParent.Care

**Palette:** Honey #C58F3D, warm mid-slate #3D5263, cream #F4ECD8. No clinical blue. No funereal black. No medical green. (The palette is locked even if the imprint name changes.)

**Visual register:** Hand-drawn painterly 2D. Soft brushwork. References: Cartoon Saloon "Late Afternoon," Bruno Collet "Memorable," Rauch Brothers StoryCorps Shorts (PBS). Target a low-frame-cadence feeling (6 fps perceived, per the Rauch Brothers approach). Never photoreal. Never faces in full close-up (hands, backs, shoulders, objects carry the emotion).

**Tonal register:** Modern Loss editorial voice (Rebecca Soffer / Gabrielle Birkner). Witty, frank, never bleak. Sharp humor welcome. Knowing exhausted humor welcome. Cruelty is not. The reader/viewer is already exhausted.

**Voice rules:** Follow `content_engine_voice.md` (Gawande/Didion model). Banned words, anti-AI register, no em dashes, nurturing but direct. For VO scripts specifically: 130-145 words per minute, silences of 0.6-1.2s on heavy beats. The image is wallpaper for the voice. The voice carries the emotion, never the AI visuals.

**What this is NOT:** Pharma ad. Documentary. Content farm. AI slop. Inspirational-quote-over-stock-photo. UGC pretending to be real. It is scripted dramatized fiction that tells emotional truth without exploiting real people.

## 2. Transparency Framework {#transparency}

This is non-negotiable. Every piece of Honey Hour content carries:

**On-screen:** "A [IMPRINT] Story. A Dramatization." in captions and/or intertitle.
**In descriptions:** "This is the first episode of [series name], a short animated series from [IMPRINT]."
**In credits:** "Animation: [IMPRINT] with [tools used]"

Never pass AI content as authentic UGC. Never imply real people are depicted. The ethical advantage of fictional dramatization is that AgingParent.Care can tell emotional truth without the consent problems that plague documentary caregiver content on TikTok.

**If viewer comments include "wait, is this real?" more than 5% of the time:** increase dramatization signals (more intertitles, stronger voiceover framing, explicit "A Dramatization" tag on-screen).

## 3. The 8 Content Buckets {#buckets}

Each bucket is a modality with different production requirements, psychological triggers, and funnel positions. When planning a content batch, select across multiple buckets for genuine creative variety (this is what Spencer Pawliw's framework demands: meaningfully different creatives, not rewords).

### Bucket A: Reels Sketches (under 60s, single-scene, twist or gut punch)
- **Triggers:** Identity (Layer 3), Curiosity Trap, Social Currency
- **Format:** Visual + VO, no dialogue, no lip sync needed
- **AI strengths:** Still life, controlled palette, no faces required
- **Examples:** "The Refrigerator," "Three Words," "Things My Mother Says"
- **Funnel position:** Top-of-funnel awareness, saves + shares
- **Production:** Cheapest bucket. 1-3 AI stills, minimal motion, strong caption track.
- **CTA pattern:** End card with comment trigger ("Comment ROUTINE for the link")

### Bucket B: Recurring Character Series (1-3 min episodic)
- **Triggers:** Story Hijack, Identity, Format Recognition
- **Format:** Character-driven scenes, VO + limited character motion
- **AI strengths:** Stable sets, same-character continuity via Soul ID
- **Examples:** "Mom and the Three of Us" (Eleanor), "Dad Doesn't Know He's the Patient" (Hank), "Dinner With Mom"
- **Funnel position:** Mid-funnel, builds return viewership and community
- **Production:** Requires Soul ID character training. Higher investment but compounds.
- **CTA pattern:** Episode end card, "Episodes drop Tuesdays"

### Bucket C: Scenario Reenactments (1-3 min, familiar caregiving set pieces)
- **Triggers:** Identity, Story Hijack, Social Currency
- **Format:** Dramatized common situations, multiple family versions
- **AI strengths:** Interior scenes, controlled lighting, emotional close-ups (hands/objects)
- **Examples:** "The Keys Conversation," "Discharge Day," "The Call from the Facility"
- **Funnel position:** Mid-funnel, drives comments and shares ("my family did this exact thing")
- **Production:** Medium complexity. 6-12 shots, voiceover-driven.

### Bucket D: Anthology Mini-Dramas (3-8 min, YouTube)
- **Triggers:** Story Hijack, Curiosity Trap, Identity
- **Format:** Full three-act structure, new family each episode, voiceover spine
- **AI strengths:** Painterly memory sequences, dream logic, object close-ups
- **Examples:** "The Honey Hour" anthology, "Last Things First" (document-centered)
- **Funnel position:** Bottom-funnel, product integration through story (the $97 System dramatized as fiction)
- **Production:** Highest investment per episode. 15-22 shots, hired VO, full post in DaVinci Resolve.
- **CTA pattern:** Soft product mention earned through story, newsletter signup

### Bucket E: Genre Play (familiar moments staged as another genre)
- **Triggers:** Format Recognition (the genre format), Social Currency (shareable because funny), Curiosity Trap
- **Format:** Recognizable genre conventions applied to caregiving: heist, courtroom drama, noir, romcom
- **AI strengths:** Stylized lighting, genre-specific visual language
- **Examples:** "The Heist" (medication routine as Ocean's Eleven), "Noir: Who Moved Mom's Glasses"
- **Funnel position:** Top-of-funnel breakout potential. The Heist is the predicted breakout Reel.
- **Production:** Medium. Heavy on editing rhythm, sound design. Genre-specific visual prompts.
- **CTA pattern:** Punchline + heart + memorable format + comment trigger

### Bucket F: Voiceover-Driven (visual stylized, narration carries the spine)
- **Triggers:** Story Hijack (neural coupling through voice), Identity, Curiosity Trap
- **Format:** StoryCorps Shorts model. VO reads a letter/reflection while AI animation illustrates.
- **AI strengths:** Covers every AI weakness (no lip sync, no faces, no realistic motion). The voice does the work.
- **Examples:** "Letters I Didn't Send" (crowdsourceable series, newsletter funnel)
- **Funnel position:** Full-funnel. Prestige + newsletter signup + user-generated letter submissions
- **Production:** Lowest technical risk for AI video. Highest emotional ceiling. The flagship format.
- **CTA pattern:** "Send us your letter. Anonymous or not. AgingParent.Care/letters"

### Bucket G: Interior Monologue / Dream Logic (where AI excels)
- **Triggers:** Curiosity Trap (surreal visuals), Identity, Story Hijack
- **Format:** Surreal, wordless or minimal-word, morphing environments
- **AI strengths:** AI is uniquely strong at morphing dreamscapes, environment transitions, surreal sequences. Live action would be expensive.
- **Examples:** "The Second Shift" (office morphs to mom's living room to hospital to teenager's bedroom)
- **Funnel position:** Brand prestige. Screenshot-worthy for Pinterest. Signals APC is a real creative property.
- **Production:** Medium complexity but high iteration. Relies on AI's strongest capability.

### Bucket H: Humor-Led (dark comedy of caregiving)
- **Triggers:** Social Currency (humor is the #1 share trigger), Identity, Format Recognition
- **Format:** Short, punchy, recurring formats. VO + stills or simple motion.
- **AI strengths:** Stills of environments (facility lobbies, kitchen interiors), typography-heavy
- **Examples:** "Yelp Reviews of Assisted Living Facilities," "Costco Receipts: A Sandwich Generation Forensic Analysis," "The Sibling Group Text: A Translation Guide"
- **Funnel position:** Top-of-funnel, highest share potential, builds the "funny but real" brand voice
- **Production:** Cheapest. Often just stills + VO + typography.

## 4. Implementation Tiers (What Is Actually Executable Today) {#implementation-tiers}

Not all 8 buckets are equally ready. This honest assessment determines what to build first and what to defer.

### Tier 1: Execute now (proven pipeline, low risk)

| Bucket | Why it's ready | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| F: Voiceover-Driven | Full 22-shot production package exists ("Letters I Didn't Send: The Keys"). Every AI prompt written, model routing decided, VO direction, assembly guide, QC checklist. Executable in a weekend. | Higgsfield paid plan, Google Flow (Veo 3.1), DaVinci Resolve (free), VO actor or ElevenLabs |
| A: Reel Sketches | 1-3 AI stills + text overlay. Barely needs video generation. | Any image model, caption editor |
| H: Humor-Led | Typography + stills + VO. The simplest production. | Image model, VO or text-to-speech |

**Start here.** These three buckets validate whether the audience exists before you invest in character work.

### Tier 2: Build next (doable, needs a day of experimentation)

| Bucket | What needs testing | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| E: Genre Play | Editing rhythm and genre-specific sound design (heist music, noir narration). The AI stills are easy; the post-production pacing is the craft. | Medium: if the genre parody doesn't land tonally, it reads as try-hard |
| G: Dream Logic | AI is genuinely strong at morphing environments. Hitting the painterly register consistently (not photoreal) takes prompt iteration. | Medium: visual quality variance between takes |
| C: Scenario Reenactments | 6-12 shots, voiceover-driven. Similar to Bucket F but with more scene variety and some character motion. | Low-medium: basically Bucket F with more shots |

### Tier 3: Defer until audience is validated

| Bucket | Why defer | Investment required |
|---|---|---|
| B: Recurring Characters | Needs Soul ID training per character, multi-shot consistency testing, and a commitment to regular episodes. Real investment before you know if the audience will return. | Soul ID setup, 20+ reference photos per character, episode cadence commitment |
| D: Anthology Mini-Dramas | 15-22 shots, hired VO ($300-700), full post in Resolve. Most expensive per-episode format. | Full production weekend per episode, VO budget |

**Tier 3 buckets are the goal, not the starting point.** They compound (recurring characters build community, anthology series build brand prestige), but they require proof that Tiers 1-2 found an audience first.

### The honest implementation sequence

```
Week 1-2:  Bucket A (1 Reel sketch) + Bucket F ("Letters" pilot) + Bucket H (1 humor piece)
           → 3 pieces across 3 modalities. Tests concept, not production.
Week 3-4:  Read metrics. Did anything get saves/shares/comments?
           If yes → add Bucket E ("The Heist") + Bucket G ("The Second Shift")
           If no  → retest with different concepts in Buckets A/F/H before expanding
Week 5-8:  With 2+ validated formats, apply 3:2:2 batching across winning buckets
Month 3+:  If a concept breaks through, invest in Bucket B (recurring character)
Month 6+:  If recurring character builds community, develop Bucket D (anthology)
```

This sequence means you spend under $50 in AI credits and zero VO budget before you have any data. The expensive production only kicks in after validation.

## 5. APC Layer 3 Identity Map {#layer3}

Every APC script must operate at Means-End Chain Layer 3. Here is how Layer 3 maps for the sandwich generation audience:

| Layer 1 (Product) | Layer 2 (Function) | Layer 3 (Identity/Emotion) |
|---|---|---|
| Caregiving resource guide | Organizes decisions | "I am not failing at this. I have a plan." |
| Advance directive template | Legal paperwork done | "I am the responsible one. I handled it so nobody else had to." |
| Medication tracker | Pills managed | "I am keeping them safe. I am a good daughter/son." |
| Newsletter about caregiving | Weekly information | "I am not alone in this. Someone understands." |
| Video about taking the keys | Driving safety addressed | "Someone else went through this exact thing. I'm not the villain." |
| Community of caregivers | Peer support | "I belong somewhere. This impossible thing is shared." |

**The core APC identities to target (pick one per script):**
1. "I am not failing at this" (competence under pressure)
2. "I am not alone in this" (recognition, companionship)
3. "I am the one who showed up" (duty, pride, exhaustion)
4. "I am allowed to be angry about this" (permission to feel)
5. "I can laugh about this without being heartless" (dark humor as coping)
6. "I am becoming my parent, and that is both beautiful and terrifying" (role reversal)

## 6. Sensitivity Guardrails {#guardrails}

Caregiving content touches health, grief, family conflict, cognitive decline, and end of life. These guardrails are mandatory.

### Must always:
- Give the aging parent agency, interiority, humor, and the occasional last word. Never make them a prop.
- Use specific, lived detail (twelve jars of mayonnaise, the sympathy card from 2019). Never stock language.
- Frame information as narrative, never as bullet points spoken to camera.
- Have scripts touching medical/legal documents reviewed for technical accuracy.
- Honor dark comedy where it naturally lives. The audience uses humor to cope.

### Must never:
- Center the heroic adult child saving the helpless parent (saviorism).
- Use funereal tones, clinical blue lighting, or pharmaceutical-ad sentimentality.
- Lecture. No "you are not alone." No "treasure every moment." No moralizing. No religious framing.
- Use words banned in `content_engine_voice.md`: "journey," "navigate," "delve," "crucial," "comprehensive," "landscape," etc.
- Use em dashes anywhere (on-screen, descriptions, scripts).
- Mock or ridicule cognitive decline or physical frailty.
- Film/depict actual vulnerable elders (the fictional dramatization IS the ethical choice).
- Treat dementia symptoms as comedy punchlines (confused behavior can be portrayed gently, never as the joke itself).

### The "five tests" before publishing:
1. **"Is this AI slop?"** Does every shot look hand-drawn painterly, or does any read as AI-photoreal?
2. **"Does this feel funereal?"** Any clinical blue, medical green, or grey-on-grey?
3. **"Is anyone preached at?"** Does the VO ever sound like marketing, grief counseling, or a greeting card?
4. **"Are we earning this?"** Is the music doing emotional work the voice should be doing?
5. **"Would this make my mom a prop?"** Does the elder character have a line, a gesture, a moment of agency?

## 7. Production Pipeline by Modality {#pipeline}

### Two production tracks

**Track 1: UGC/Talking-head (Sarah avatar pipeline)**
For direct-to-camera content where a spokesperson delivers practical advice.
- Uses the existing Sarah avatar + Cassidy voice pipeline (see `project_apc_video_pipeline.md`)
- ElevenLabs TTS (Cassidy) > Seedance 2.0 SILENT > Sync Labs sync-3 lip sync > ffmpeg
- Best for: practical tips, list-based content, product walkthroughs, FAQ answers
- NOT for Honey Hour Stories (different visual register entirely)

**Track 2: Honey Hour Stories (painterly dramatization pipeline)**
For all 8 buckets of scripted dramatized content.

**Image generation (stills):**
- Soul Cinema Preview: default for painterly keyframes, character shots using Soul ID
- Nano Banana Pro: shots needing legible text (legal pads, labels, signage). Unlimited on paid Higgsfield plans.
- Soul ID character training: 20+ reference photos per character, varied angles. Only train characters whose faces are shown. Never show full face close-ups; Soul ID carries hands, silhouettes, partial views.

**Video generation (motion):**
- Higgsfield DoP I2V: workhorse for 5-second painterly camera moves from stills. 50+ motion presets (Slow Push In, Subtle Forward Drift, etc.). No audio output.
- Veo 3.1 Fast: atmospheric shots needing native ambient audio (hospital hum, cardinal call, car engine). ~$0.15/sec. Up to 8s per clip.
- Veo 3.1 Standard: hero shots only (rack focus, complex motion). ~$0.40/sec. One or two per episode max.
- Kling 3.0: character-driven motion with cinematic depth. Best slow pushes and rack focus. ~6 credits/clip on Higgsfield.
- Seedance 2.0: cheap multi-shot drafts, longest single clips (15s). Good for iterating before committing Kling/Veo credits.

**Audio:**
- VO: Hire human narrator via Artlist Pro or Voices.com ($300-700 per episode). ElevenLabs Eleven v3 (stability 0.35, similarity 0.80, style 0.04) as fallback only.
- Music: Artlist Pro. Solo piano + cello. Download with stems for dynamic mapping. Composer refs: Dustin O'Halloran, Olafur Arnalds, Hauschka, Goldmund.
- SFX: Artlist SFX library. Diegetic only (pen on paper, cardinal call, car settling).
- Room tone: Kitchen room tone, hospital ambience, both at -36 dB.
- Veo native audio: ambient beds only, NEVER for voiceover.

**Post-production:**
- DaVinci Resolve 20 (free). Fairlight for VO leveling. Color page for Honey Hour palette grade.
- "HoneyHour_v1" Power Grade: gamma warm toward 7000K, highlights toward honey, shadows toward warm slate, max 5% shift.
- VO at -16 LUFS, music at -20 to -22 LUFS (peak -16 only at emotional climax), SFX at -28 to -22, room tone at -36 to -42. Total mix at -14 LUFS.

**Model routing decision tree:**
```
What does the shot need?
  Painterly still with no motion → Soul Cinema still, Ken-Burns in Resolve
  Painterly still with slow camera move → Soul Cinema still > DoP I2V
  Legible text in frame → Nano Banana Pro still > DoP I2V
  Ambient audio (birds, hospital, engine) → Veo 3.1 Fast
  Complex camera move (rack focus) → Veo 3.1 Standard (budget max 1-2 per episode)
  Character close-up with motion → Kling 3.0 from Soul ID still
  Cheap draft / iteration → Seedance 2.0
  Title/CTA cards → DaVinci Resolve (never AI)
```

## 8. Scale Execution Playbook {#scale}

### Weekly production cadence (Stage 1: first 60 days)

**Week 1-2: Prove the format**
- Produce Pilot A: "The Heist" (Bucket E, 60s Reel). Cheapest, fastest, tests genre-play resonance.
- Produce Pilot D: "The Second Shift" (Bucket G, 90s Reel). Tests dream-logic modality.
- Produce Pilot B: "Letters I Didn't Send: The Keys" (Bucket F, 4min YouTube). Lowest technical risk, highest emotional ceiling.

**Week 3-8: Read metrics, decide**
- Decision threshold at 14 days (YouTube) / 7 days (Reels):
  - Greenlight: avg view duration 45%+ on YouTube AND 1+ signup per 200 viewers
  - Iterate: avg view duration 30-44%, signups 0.2-0.5%
  - Scrap format: avg view duration below 30% AND signups below 0.1%

### Batch production methodology (Stage 2: days 60-180)

Apply Spencer Pawliw's 3:2:2 per winning concept from Stage 1:
- 3 creative variations across different buckets (e.g., the "keys" concept as Bucket C reenactment, Bucket F letter, Bucket E genre play)
- 2 hooks (different triggers from the 6)
- 2 body variants (different story structures)

**Weekly rhythm once validated:**
- Monday: Script 2 new pieces (1 Reel-length, 1 YouTube-length). Apply the script output format from Part 2 Phase 3 of the main skill.
- Tuesday: Generate stills and train any new Soul IDs.
- Wednesday: Generate motion clips, commission/generate VO.
- Thursday: Assembly in Resolve, QC checklist.
- Friday: Stage publishes. Reels on Wednesday 7pm ET, YouTube on Wednesday 7pm ET, Pinterest pin on Sunday 8pm ET.

**Monthly output target (once rhythm is established):**
- 4 YouTube episodes (Buckets B, C, D, or F)
- 8 Reels (Buckets A, E, G, H)
- Total: ~12 pieces/month across multiple modalities

### Stage 3 (days 180+): Productize
- Reuse top Reels as Pinterest video pins (1000x1500 vertical, 2:3 ratio)
- Bundle recurring video content into the $97 Complete System as free tasters
- If a recurring character breaks out (likely Hank from Bucket B), develop a 10-episode YouTube season
- Launch "Letters I Didn't Send" submission portal at AgingParent.Care/letters as newsletter funnel

### Content batch planning template

When planning a batch, fill this out:

```
BATCH: [Month/Week]
CONCEPT: [Core Layer 3 identity being tested]
BUCKET A (Reel): [title] - [trigger] - [CTA]
BUCKET E (Genre): [title] - [genre] - [trigger] - [CTA]
```

## 9. Performance Benchmarks {#benchmarks}

### Organic metrics (no paid media)

| Metric | Signal | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Saves (Reels) | Strongest resonance | Content is "private medicine" (meaningful to the individual) |
| Shares (Reels) | Social Currency working | Content passes the Wingman Test |
| Comments with personal disclosure | Identity trigger hit | "This made me think about my dad" > "Beautiful" |
| Avg view duration 45%+ (YouTube) | Story Hijack working | Neural coupling is holding |
| Newsletter signup 0.5%+ | Funnel working | CTA earned through story, not forced |
| "Is this real?" comments < 5% | Transparency working | Dramatization signals are adequate |
| "Is this AI?" comments negative | Visual register failing | Retreat to more painterly, less photoreal |

### Diagnostic flow (APC-specific)

```
High saves, low shares       → Private medicine, not shareable. Test humor angles (Bucket H).
High shares, low saves       → Social Currency works but content is disposable. Add depth.
High comments, low signups   → CTA is weak. Test stronger end cards, QR codes.
Everything low               → Concept missed. Back to Layer 3 research.
"AI slop" comments           → Increase stylization. More painterly, more VO-driven.
                               If persistent, retreat to Bucket F (voiceover + stills).
```

### Fallback strategy

If AI video tools degrade (more "slop" criticism, platform deboosting):
- Retreat to StoryCorps model: voiceover + mostly still or near-still imagery (Bucket F)
- This format survives any AI quality regression
- The voice carries the spine; the image is wallpaper

If a competitor (AARP, A Place for Mom, Daughterhood) launches a scripted property:
- Accelerate recurring-character work (Buckets B, D)
- Specificity is the moat. "Hank's lamp" beats "generic caregiver content."
