
# Creative Engine Skill

## Core doctrine: ugly ads beat polished

**Barry Hott's thesis, backed by $600M+ in managed spend across True Classic, AG1, Harry's, Tushy**: polished ads look like ads, users skip ads. Raw UGC and "ugly statics" with handwritten-style text overlays convert better because they look organic, not branded.

Documented case: one of Barry Hott's students saw ROAS increase 72% and revenue 90% within 2-3 days of switching from polished studio creative to relatable UGC.

This is the dominant creative doctrine across Nick Shackelford, Taylor Holiday, Andrew Foxwell, and Nick Theriot as of 2026. It is the opposite of the 2019-era "high production value" doctrine most beginner dropshipping courses still teach.

**Practical implication:** stop paying $500 for polished studio videos. Spend $200 on 10 Fiverr UGCs and test them all.

## Format rules for 2026

| Spec | Rule |
|---|---|
| Primary aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical (Reels, Stories placements) |
| Secondary | 4:5 for feed when 9:16 crops awkwardly |
| Legacy | 1:1 (only if needed for old placements) |
| Hook window | First 1 second (Meta thumb-stop metric, not 3-second view) |
| Audio | Assume muted; text overlay must communicate hook |
| Text overlay | Large, handwritten-feel or bold sans, NOT corporate |
| Length | 15 to 30 seconds for video; statics are equally viable |
| Captions | Always burned in, not Meta auto-caption |

## The 5 winning creative formats (2026)

1. **POV / first-person demo**: "I tried this for 30 days and..."
2. **Before / after split-screen**: problem state on left, solution state on right. Avoid in regulated categories (health, weight loss).
3. **Problem / solution narrator**: talking head UGC walks through the pain and the product's answer
4. **Reaction / reveal**: "Look what just showed up", unboxing style
5. **Ugly static**: product photo + handwritten text overlay, looks like a meme not an ad

## Hook library (test 3-5 per launch)

The hook is the first 1-3 seconds. Variance here is 3-10x on performance. Test aggressively.

**Problem agitation hooks:**
- "If you [pain point], watch this."
- "I've tried everything for [problem] and nothing worked until..."
- "POV: you finally found the fix for [problem]."

**Curiosity hooks:**
- "Why didn't anyone tell me about this sooner?"
- "This is what Amazon doesn't want you to know."
- "I found this on TikTok and I'm obsessed."

**Social proof hooks:**
- "[Number] people bought this in 24 hours."
- "My [friend/mom/roommate] won't stop talking about this."
- "The [product] that's going viral on TikTok."

**Demonstration hooks:**
- Visual of problem (cluttered desk, bad posture, messy kitchen) + product appears
- Unexpected use case demo in first 2 seconds
- Contrast / transformation in first 3 seconds

**Hooks to AVOID** (common rookie mistakes):
- Product name in the first 3 seconds (nobody cares what it's called yet)
- Logo or branded intro (signals ad, loses thumb-stop)
- "Are you tired of..." (overused, audience trained to skip)
- Corporate voiceover (immediately reads as ad)

| Platform | Price range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fiverr | $10-$50/video | Cheap, inconsistent quality; buy in bulk, test all |
| Billo | $75-$150/video | Standardized process, solid baseline quality |

| Collabstr | $100-$500/video | Curated US-based creators |

**The 2026 operator pattern:** buy 10 Fiverr UGCs for $200, run as ABO creative tests. For winners (ad sets that cleared kill criteria), invest $150 in a Billo or Insense UGC to scale with.

### AI UGC (use with caution)

Tools: Arcads, Pippit, HeyGen.

**Pros:** 50+ variants in hours, unlimited A/B testing.
**Cons:** Barry Hott and Taylor Holiday both warn against AI UGC for brand building; authenticity signal suppressed. Meta also increasingly detects and penalizes obvious AI avatars in some placements.

**Practical stance:** use AI UGC for iteration tests (cheap volume), not as the hero creative for your scaling ad. Scale with real UGC.

## Iteration cadence

| Operation level | New creatives per product per week |
|---|---|
| Minimum viable | 5 |
| Scaling operator | 15 to 40 |
| Seven-figure agency | 60+ |

**Test priority (highest variance first):**
1. Hooks: 3-10x performance swing. Test 3-5 per launch.
2. Angles: 2-5x swing. Example angles for a posture corrector: office worker, gym rat, older adult, athlete recovery, chronic pain.
3. CTAs: 1.3-2x swing. "Shop now" vs "Get yours" vs "Try it" vs "See why".
4. Format / aspect ratio: 1.2-1.5x swing. Test last.

## Creative fatigue and rotation

Winning creative lifespan in 2026: 7 to 21 days. Then CTR decays.

**Practical rotation rule:** when CTR drops 30% from launch CTR, retire and replace. Do not "give it another week".

**Fatigue symptoms:**
- CTR trending down for 3+ days
- Frequency above 3.5 with flat purchases
- CPM climbing without spend increase
- Comment quality decaying (trolls, irrelevant)

**Fresh angle > fresh hook > fresh cut.** When a creative fatigues, the strongest move is a NEW angle, not a new hook on the same angle.

## Writing the creative brief

When working with a UGC creator or producing in-house, the brief should be under 200 words and look like this:

```
PRODUCT: [name + 1-sentence description]
ANGLE: [ICP + pain point + promise]
HOOK (first 1-3 seconds): [exact line or visual direction]
CORE MESSAGE (seconds 3-15): [bullet list of 2-3 points]
CTA (last 3 seconds): [exact line]
FORMAT: 9:16, 20-30 seconds, burned-in captions, natural phone lighting
TONE: relatable, unpolished, do NOT sound like an ad
REFERENCE ADS: [2-3 Meta Ad Library URLs of winners in this category]
DELIVERABLE: raw .mp4, un-edited bonus B-roll welcome
```

Never give a UGC creator a script longer than 150 words. Let them improvise.

## AIDA and 5-stage-awareness framing

The 5 Stages of Market Awareness (Eugene Schwartz, popularized by Nick Theriot):

1. **Unaware**: doesn't know they have the problem. Hard ads, requires pattern interrupt.
2. **Problem aware**: knows the pain, not the solution. Creative should name the pain sharply.
3. **Solution aware**: knows categories of solutions exist. Differentiate your product from generic.
4. **Product aware**: knows your product exists. Social proof and price/offer.
5. **Most aware**: ready to buy. Direct CTA, urgency.

**For cold Meta traffic on a new product**, you are almost always targeting stages 2 and 3. Write for them specifically. Don't make the mistake of writing stage-5 "limited time offer" creative to stage-2 audiences.

## Meta's Andromeda (2024-2026 algorithm shift)

Meta rolled out the Andromeda ranking model that rewards creative diversity within a campaign. Practical implication: the account with 40 different creatives beats the account with 4 excellent creatives, because Andromeda uses the variance to identify micro-audience fits.

This cements creative volume as the dominant lever, not targeting.

## Policy-safe creative checklist

Before shipping any creative, verify:

- [ ] No second-person "you" implying a personal characteristic
- [ ] No before/after imagery in regulated categories
- [ ] No medical/health claims without substantiation
- [ ] No trademarked imagery, logos, celebrity likeness
- [ ] No "guaranteed", "proven", "clinically tested" without proof
- [ ] No shock/gore/injury imagery
- [ ] No income or weight loss promises
- [ ] Text overlay in image is under 20% of frame (old rule still sometimes enforced)

## Do not

- Recommend polished studio production as the default. It is the 2019 playbook.
- Treat hooks as interchangeable. Hooks have 3-10x variance; test more hooks than anything else.
- Skip creator briefs to "let them improvise". Improvisation needs constraints.
- Keep a fatigued creative alive past the 30% CTR-drop rule. It is stealing budget from fresh tests.
- Use AI UGC as the hero creative in a scaling campaign. It suppresses authenticity signal.
- Use stage-5 "buy now" creative on stage-2 cold traffic. Mismatch kills conversion.
